About Data Darbar

An open explorer of Pakistan's official statistics, by place, by sector and by institution.

Our ambition

Our ambition is to build an open, harmonised research-data infrastructure for Pakistan — a shared foundation for studying the country's economy, society and local development.

We are working towards linking data across sources and years through consistent geographical identifiers, documented boundary changes and reproducible methods. The aim is to let researchers, journalists and the public spend more time answering questions and less time rebuilding the data, with clear guidance on where comparisons are reliable, approximate or not supported.

The project

Data Darbar brings Pakistan's official statistics together in one place: the population census, household and labour-force surveys, the national accounts, 8-digit trade data, the federal budget and tax collection, the State Bank's monetary and external series, and what the courts, the police and the power regulator report about themselves. The sources are published by the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics, the Finance Division, the State Bank of Pakistan, the Law and Justice Commission, provincial police and NEPRA, and most of them are PDF tables and separate reports. The name is a nod to the shrine in Lahore, reimagined as a place of gathering for Pakistan's data.

Every figure is traceable to its published source, every chart downloads as CSV, and the cleaned tables behind the site are published in full rather than locked inside PDFs.

What is here

  • Places: every district and tehsil on the PBS Census 2023 boundaries, with census, survey, poverty, satellite, rural-facility and crop indicators on one map, and the 2017 and 2023 censuses side by side.
  • Economy: output and growth since 1951, industry, trade by product and partner, prices and interest rates, the rupee, remittances and the external balance.
  • State: the federal budget and tax collection, court case flows and judges, reported crime, power plants and electricity distribution, and disasters.
  • For analysts: the data catalogue documents every table, field, period and join key, with downloads; Query runs SQL over the same tables in your browser, with nothing sent to a server.

How it is built

Data Darbar is built from a reproducible pipeline: raw PBS and Finance Division publications (PDF tables parsed programmatically, CSV releases and survey microdata) and State Bank series fetched from its API are cleaned and normalised into a DuckDB + Parquet warehouse, then exported as the compact data files the site loads. The notes below cover each view in turn.

Where each figure comes from, how places are matched across boundary changes and what each source will and will not support is set out in Methods.

Reuse

The code is MIT-licensed and the derived data is CC BY 4.0, with one exception: the OpenStreetMap health amenities table is redistributed under the Open Database Licence (ODbL) 1.0, which is share-alike, and its catalogue page says so. The 130 Islamabad rows of the school layer are also from OpenStreetMap and stay under the same licence. Cite Data Darbar and the original source named on each chart or in the catalogue.

Built by

Hiba Sameen is an economist and data scientist based in London. She holds a PhD in Economics and has worked across academia, government and think tanks on economic policy, with an interest in data science and engineering focused on making public data more accessible. Data Darbar grew out of the frustration of comparing Pakistani statistics that are scattered across PDF tables and separate reports.
