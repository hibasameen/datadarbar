Output, income and the rupee — 1999-2000 onwards

The four numbers the rest of this page is measured against. Indexed puts them all at 100 in 1999-2000, so their paths can be compared; Levels gives each its own units and axis.
Three aggregates, and they are not the same number. GVA at basic prices is what the sector charts on this page add up to; GDP at market prices is GVA plus taxes less subsidies; GNI is GDP plus net primary income from abroad, which is largely remittances and was negative until 2001-02. For 2025-26 they are Rs 42.6tn, Rs 45.8tn and Rs 49.8tn. All three are at constant 2015-16 prices, from PBS National Accounts Table 5, and both identities hold to the rupee in every year. Income per person is shown on two population bases. From 2023-24 PBS reprojects population on the 2023 Census, so the two series are not a continuation of one another — on the 2017-Census basis 2025-26 is Rs 203,118, on the 2023-Census basis Rs 195,484 — and joining them would draw a fall that is a change of denominator, not of income. The exchange rate is the annual average.

The economy quarter by quarter — value added, Rs trillion at 2015-16 prices

Pakistan’s only quarterly output series, 2015-16 to 2024-25. Hover a quarter for its composition.
Pakistan’s only quarterly output series. Between January–March and April–June 2020 industry fell from Rs 1.70tn to Rs 1.36tn and services from Rs 5.13tn to Rs 4.79tn, while agriculture rose from Rs 1.99tn to Rs 2.05tn. These are unadjusted sequential quarters and are not seasonally adjusted, so a quarter-on-quarter move is not on its own a measure of what any one event did. Quarters are fiscal: Q1 is July–September. The four quarters sum to the annual GVA — not to GDP, which adds taxes less subsidies — and they agree with Table 5 to within Rs 2 million in eight of the ten years. Two do not: 2023-24 by Rs 15.9bn and 2024-25 by Rs 72.0bn, where the quarterly release and the annual table are different vintages.

Composition of growth — contributions to real GDP growth, percentage points

Each year's growth split into the parts each sector supplied: last year's share of the economy multiplied by this year's real growth. Bars above zero added to growth, below zero subtracted; the black line is headline GDP growth. Click a year to break it down below.
A decomposition, not a causal story: it says where growth showed up, not what caused it. Each activity’s contribution is its own change in output over last year’s total, both at constant 2015-16 prices, so the parts sum to the headline exactly rather than approximately — to within a billionth of a percentage point, which the build checks and fails on. Eighteen activities, none of them inside another: the earlier version listed Crops beside Cotton Ginning, which is one of the three things Crops is made of, and multiplied real growth rates by current-price shares, so in 2022-23 its parts came to −0.93 points against a headline of −0.21. The headline here is growth in gross value added at basic prices, which is what these activities add up to; GDP adds taxes less subsidies on top and is a slightly different number. Available from 2000-01, the first year with a previous year to compare against.

How registered manufacturing changed between two censuses — CMI 2005-06 vs 2015-16

The Census of Manufacturing Industries counts every registered factory (10+ employees) roughly once a decade. Between the last two rounds, reporting establishments went from 8,680 to 42,578 and employment from 1.10 m to 2.34 m. Each row shows an industry's share of the manufacturing total in 2005-06 and in 2015-16.
Shares of all manufacturing, so the two censuses stay comparable despite the large jump in coverage. The textile story is the headline: 46% → 30% of factory employment and 27% → 20% of value added between 2005-06 and 2015-16 — while non-metallic minerals (cement, bricks) and apparel surged. Hover for absolute numbers. The 2015-16 round is the most recent census PBS has published.

The rupee against the dollar — rupees per US$, monthly

Every month since August 1947. The rupee has lost about 98.8% of its dollar value over that span, but almost none of it happened smoothly.
State Bank of Pakistan, bank floating average exchange rates. Rates before 1982 are the managed peg, so the flat stretches are policy, not market calm.

The real trade-weighted rupee — effective exchange rate indices, 2010 = 100

The nominal effective rate (NEER) tracks the rupee against a trade-weighted basket. The real rate (REER) adjusts for inflation differences. Both are indices against their own 2010 base: above 100 means the rupee is dearer than it was in 2010 in real terms, which makes exports harder than they were then. It does not mean the rupee is overvalued: an index measures distance from its own base year, not from a fair value, and nothing on this page estimates one.
SBP, nominal and real effective exchange rate indices (base 2010), monthly from July 2001.

Where the dollars come from, and where they go — the current account, US$

Every dollar Pakistan earns from the rest of the world on the left; every dollar it spends on the right. The boxes are drawn to scale, so when the right-hand side is wider the country ran a current-account deficit that year and had to borrow or draw down reserves to cover it. Use the Summary / Groups / Detail / Everything toggle to break the boxes down in place — each subdivides inside its own area, so the two sides stay comparable — or click any single box to open just that one (and its heading to close it). Goods go four levels deep, to individual commodities.
—
SBP, monthly balance of payments summary on the BPM6 standard, summed to fiscal years (July–June). Inflows minus outflows equal SBP’s published current-account balance to the dollar; the build checks this. “Other transfers in” is secondary income other than workers’ remittances. Breakdowns: goods from SBP’s export receipts and import payments by commodity (through banks, so before the freight adjustment — the header shows both figures), services from the BPM6 services table, remittances from the country-wise table, income paid from repatriation of profits and dividends by sector. The commodity hierarchy is not published as such; it is recovered from the numbers (a group is the series its neighbours sum to in every year) and checked against the totals.

The economy since FY50 — real GDP at 2005-06 factor cost, and its growth

Seventy years on one line. On the ratio scale an equal slope is an equal rate of growth, so the steep 1960s and the flatter stretches after them can be compared directly. The level is the State Bank’s own splice of every PBS base onto 2005-06 prices; the growth rate is the one it prints beside it.
State Bank of Pakistan, Handbook of Statistics on Pakistan Economy 2020, table 1.5: GDP at constant factor cost, FY50 to FY21, as published. Fiscal years are named by the year they end (FY50 = 1949-50).

Money and prices since 1950 — % change on a year earlier

Broad money against consumer prices. Money growing much faster than output tends to show up later in prices; the 1970s and the years after 2018 are where the two lines rise together. The line breaks wherever the comparison would not be like with like: where the State Bank changes the definition of money (1986, 1991 and 2005), in 1972, when the figures stop including East Pakistan, and wherever the price index changes its base.
State Bank of Pakistan, Handbook of Statistics on Pakistan Economy 2020: table 4.1 (monetary statistics, from 1950) and table 2.8 (consumer price index, from FY50). The growth rates are computed by Data Darbar from SBP’s levels and indices, within each block and base. The current money series, from 2006, is under Money & banks.

How much of the trade the detail accounts for — parts as % of the published total

Every other chart on this page is built by adding up parts — partners, or commodity groups. This is how close those parts come to the totals PBS publishes separately. 100% means nothing is missing.
Read the partner charts for the 2000s with this in front of you. Before 2011-12 the country tables account for only 85 to 91 per cent of imports: between a tenth and a seventh of everything Pakistan bought in those years is not attributed to any country, so a chart of its largest partners is really a chart of the partners PBS managed to name. From 2011-12 the gap closes to under two per cent. The commodity groups add to the total in every year, so the basket charts do not carry this problem. Exports run slightly over 100 per cent in 2020-21 and 2021-22, which is the same kind of failure in the other direction. Dollar figures were not published before 2015-16, so the dollar view starts there; 2025-26 covers eleven months.
