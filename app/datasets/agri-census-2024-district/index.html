Read before using

The 7th Agricultural Census (fieldwork September 2024 to February 2025) is a weighted sample, not a count: PBS drew 11,083 mouzas and urban blocks, listed every household in them, took holdings of 100 acres or 50 cattle at 100 per cent and sub-sampled the rest, and raised the result to the frame. Every count is therefore a fraction, the design aims at a 15 per cent margin of error at district level, and no sampling errors are published. Treat shares and per-farm rates as the usable figures and the counts as approximate. Where the farm count is below 5,000 (farms_implausible), the weighting has plainly gone wrong — Umerkot at 2,098 farms on 210,000 acres, Kech, Nushki, Awaran, Duki, Chagai, Hyderabad and the Karachi districts — and per-farm rates should not be used; Khyber, Harnai and Mohmand, with more farms than one and a half times their rural households in the 2023 census (Khyber 756,000 farms averaging 0.4 acres), are the same problem in the other direction and carry the same flag. rural_hh is the Census 2023 denominator used for the test. Sindh’s district farm counts are unreliable well beyond that flag. In Table 1.1 a size class’s farm area divided by its farms has to fall inside the class: a farm in the 1.0 to 2.5 acre class cannot average 26 acres, as Umerkot’s does (Tando Muhammad Khan 29, Hyderabad 20; Jacobabad 0.3, Korangi 0.01). Where it does not, the counts and the areas were raised with different weights. Of Sindh’s 30 districts, 21 fail in three or more of the nine classes (5 per cent tolerance, classes of fewer than 20 farms not tested). Counts look too low against their areas in Badin, Hyderabad, Karachi West, Mirpur Khas, Sanghar, Sukkur, Tando Allahyar, Tando Muhammad Khan, Thatta and Umerkot, and too high in Jacobabad, Kambar Shahdad Kot, Karachi Central, Karachi East, Karachi South, Kashmore, Korangi, Larkana and Malir; in the 2.5 to 12.5 acre classes Naushahro Feroze’s look too low and Tharparkar’s too high. Dadu, Ghotki, Jamshoro, Keamari, Khairpur, Matiari, Shaheed Benazirabad, Shikarpur and Sujawal pass. The large-farm classes (25 acres and above) pass in 27 of the 30, so the fault is mainly in how the sampled small holdings were raised. Sindh’s province rows are consistent: the district errors offset within the province. PBS’s own Sindh report records inconsistencies in farm numbers against earlier censuses that required revalidation (section 16.1), and its internal review of 24 July 2025 recommended further analysis of Sindh’s farm-size distribution (section 17.3). In Sindh, use farm areas and the province figures; do not rank or compare districts on farm counts, average farm size or any per-farm rate. The check is Data Darbar’s, on Table 1.1 as published (1 November 2026). The geography is PBS’s 2023 district frame, joining the geography layer on district_code; Cholistan Area and Malakand Protected Area are published as units of their own and carry no code; Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Jammu & Kashmir were enumerated but are in none of the published tables, so Pakistan is the four provinces plus Islamabad. Crop areas here are what farmers reported and run well above the Crop Reporting Service series in crops_district_fy (wheat 14.4 against 9.1 million hectares nationally); the two are different instruments and should not be read against each other. The <crop>_farms, <crop>_area_ac and <crop>_irrigated_ac columns are Chapter 6 as published, for wheat, paddy, cotton, maize, sugarcane, potatoes, oilseeds, fodders, vegetables and orchards; in Ziarat, Chaman and Killa Abdullah PBS’s orchard table reports more farms with orchards than farms, so that share exceeds 100 per cent as published. PBS omits rows that would be zero, so a missing figure is usually a zero; Washuk’s row is missing from the acre table alone and is filled from the hectare table. Areas are in acres as PBS gives them.
