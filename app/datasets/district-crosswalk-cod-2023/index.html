Read before using

Datasets drawn on COD-AB polygons — satellite zonal statistics, Adaad’s climate, fire, forest and flood tables — name their districts by an upper-case district_key on a 160-district frame: the 136 census districts rebuilt from COD tehsils, plus Azad Jammu & Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan as COD draws them. That key, cod_district_key here, is not Data Darbar’s district_key, and nothing on the map joins to it; district_code does. 127 districts are matched through their COD tehsil codes, which PBS’s tehsil layer carries; 29 by name, because no COD tehsil code survives there (all of AJK and GB, and districts created after 2022); and 4 by area. COD draws Gilgit-Baltistan in 14 districts and PBS in 10, so Darel and Tangir fold into Diamer, Gupis Yasin into Ghizer and Rondu into Skardu. Sum counts across a merged group; weight a mean by area_weight, or by population for anything about people. overlap_share measures each match: the two layers are different digitisations of the same borders, and the note says where they part — most in Karachi, Quetta, Killa Abdullah, Chaman and Batagram, and in Kharmang, which COD draws so much larger that 56 per cent of it lies inside PBS’s Skardu. A table keyed on COD p-codes joins on cod_adm2_pcode, except for COD’s Lehri (PK218): its two tehsils went to Kachhi and Sibi, so it has no single home.
